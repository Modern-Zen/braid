01 / The shift

Built for scarce hands.

Branches, pull requests, review requests, notifications: tools for coordinating a few people typing.

Built for scarce attention.

When agents are abundant, the bottleneck is your judgment. Headwaters spends it carefully.

02 / Five ideas

The river does the coordinating. You pick the line.

Forks, not branches.

Every attempt is its own repo with its own scoped token. Agents can't touch each other's work, or main.

Lines.

Agents declare what they'll touch before they write. Overlaps surface instantly, so conflict is avoided upstream.

Scout.

Compare every attempt running side by side: live previews, tests, cost, and a judge's verdict. Choose; don't read diffs.

Logbook.

The why behind every commit lives in the repo: plan, decisions, rejected paths. Versioned, forkable, searchable.

Eddy.

The only place a human is needed. Headwaters measures itself by how few decisions it asks of you.

03 / How it flows
Intent

One sentence about what should change.

Channels

Several agents fork the River and start at once.

Lines

Each declares the files it will touch. Overlaps surface early.

Scout

Previews, tests, cost and a verdict, side by side.

Confluence

You pick a line. Or the river picks, when it's clear.

Main

The chosen change lands, with its Logbook attached.
